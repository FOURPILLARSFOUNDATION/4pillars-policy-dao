# Automation Revenue Architecture

**Status:** scoping  
**Question:** As productivity becomes less dependent on traditional payroll, which lawful and economically sound public-revenue approaches can support public services and worker transition without freezing useful innovation?

## Candidate approaches for study

- Impact-based infrastructure charges related to extraordinary grid, water, land-use, or local-service demand, where legally authorized.
- Value-capture mechanisms tied to publicly funded infrastructure improvements.
- Broad-based tax-base modernization rather than a narrowly defined “AI tax.”
- Worker-transition, training, and local-community benefit agreements linked to major projects.

## Required analysis

Every proposal must define the tax base, jurisdiction, nexus, rate logic, incidence, exemptions, administrative cost, competitiveness effect, and use of proceeds. It must compare alternatives, including reforms to existing corporate, property, payroll, or consumption taxes.

## Guardrails

Do not assert that automation inevitably destroys a fixed quantity of jobs or that a particular technology company owes a particular charge without evidence. The objective is resilient public finance and broad opportunity, not punishment of a sector.
