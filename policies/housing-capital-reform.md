# Housing Capital Reform

**Status:** scoping  
**Question:** Can primary-residence owners access carefully limited liquidity while increasing opportunity for small investors without worsening affordability, displacement, or foreclosure risk?

## Design constraints

- A primary home is shelter first, not merely an asset class.
- Consumer losses, fees, and exit conditions must be understandable before enrollment.
- Programs must not create pressure to sell, accelerate foreclosure, or disguise predatory credit.
- Any fractional or shared-appreciation structure may trigger securities, consumer-credit, tax, title, landlord-tenant, and state real-estate-law questions.
- Participation by institutional buyers must be capped or structured so it does not crowd out households.

## Research tracks

1. Compare home-equity loans, reverse mortgages, shared-appreciation agreements, community land trusts, limited-equity cooperatives, and public revolving funds.
2. Model effects under declining prices, interest-rate shocks, death, disability, divorce, relocation, and default.
3. Require plain-language disclosures, independent counseling, cooling-off periods, data minimization, and an accessible dispute process.
4. Test whether public or nonprofit structures can better capture upside for affordability than a new speculative secondary market.

## Decision tests

A future model may advance only if evidence supports net homeowner benefit, no material increase in displacement or foreclosure risk, enforceable consumer protections, and a credible affordability impact assessment.
