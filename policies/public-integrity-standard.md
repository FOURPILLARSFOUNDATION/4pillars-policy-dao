# Public Integrity Standard

**Status:** draft  
**Purpose:** Provide a model framework to reduce actual and perceived financial conflicts where public officials exercise authority over markets, companies, contracts, or assets.

## Model policy objectives

1. Timely, standardized, machine-readable financial disclosures.
2. Clear restrictions or mandatory divestment/qualified arrangements for defined covered assets, tailored to jurisdiction and office.
3. Recusal requirements where a reasonably informed person would see a material conflict.
4. An independent enforcement office with investigatory authority, due process, published procedures, and a protected budget.
5. Proportionate civil penalties, correction orders, and public reporting.
6. Whistleblower protections and secure reporting pathways.

## Essential due-process safeguards

- Written notice of the alleged violation and relevant evidence.
- Opportunity to respond before a neutral decision-maker.
- Consistent standards, appeal rights, and judicial review where applicable.
- Clear separation between evidence, allegation, finding, and sanction.

## Data and transparency standard

The public should be able to inspect disclosures, recusals, enforcement statistics, and source records where lawful. Publishing an algorithmic “corruption score” without traceable records, error correction, and appeal rights is prohibited by project standard.

## Open questions

- Which assets and relationships should be covered by office and jurisdiction?
- What structure creates genuine independence without becoming unaccountable?
- How should privacy, security, and family interests be balanced against disclosure?
- What constitutional and statutory limits apply in each jurisdiction?
