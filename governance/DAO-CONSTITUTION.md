# 4PILLARS Policy Commons Constitution

Version 0.1 — founding draft

## Article I — Purpose and scope

4PILLARS Policy Commons is an open, nonpartisan policy-development community. Its purpose is to produce inspectable public-interest research, model policy, civic technology, and implementation learning. It is not a political party, PAC, charity, lobbyist, broker, investment fund, or legal-services provider unless and until a separate legal entity is created for that limited purpose.

## Article II — Membership

Membership recognizes contribution and participation; it confers no equity, profit right, financial return, or ownership claim. Access shall not depend on buying a token. The project may maintain verified contributor roles to protect vote integrity, but must publish the eligibility rules and appeal process.

## Article III — Member rights

Members may propose policy modules, comment on proposals, inspect non-sensitive decision records, disclose conflicts, and appeal a moderation or process decision. Members have a duty to preserve privacy, follow the Code of Conduct, and distinguish personal advocacy from a project position.

## Article IV — Decision system

### 1. Deliberation first

No material policy vote may open without a public problem brief, source list, impact assessment, and a comment period of at least 14 days, except a documented emergency administrative action.

### 2. Voting

The founding configuration uses one verified active contributor, one vote. A supermajority of two-thirds is required to adopt or materially amend a model policy, remove a steward, or change this Constitution. Participation and quorum thresholds must be published before each vote.

### 3. Delegation

Members may delegate a vote by topic, for a defined period, and revoke that delegation at any time. Delegates must disclose material conflicts. Delegation must be transparent and may not be sold, bought, or conditioned on a contribution.

### 4. Public record

Publish proposal text, version history, vote totals, conflicts disclosed by decision-makers, abstentions, and concise reasons for the decision. Redact personal data and legitimate security information.

## Article V — Stewards and working groups

Stewards administer the process; they do not own the policy. Their limited powers are to maintain repository integrity, enforce contribution standards, coordinate reviews, and execute approved administrative work. Working groups may recommend policy but cannot commit funds, conduct regulated political activity, or claim legal authority without a separate explicit mandate.

## Article VI — Conflicts, recusals, and evidence

Decision-makers must disclose material financial, employment, family, organizational, or political conflicts. A conflicted person may provide public expertise but must recuse from final review or voting when the conflict is direct and substantial. Every evidence claim should be traceable to a source or flagged as an assumption.

## Article VII — Legal and financial firewall

The Commons must keep research, governance, regulated advocacy, charitable work, lobbying, and political activity operationally distinct. If a separate entity is created, it needs its own governing instrument, counsel, accounting, compliance owner, records, and public disclosures as applicable.

No member may use the Commons to:

- coordinate expenditures with a candidate or political committee;
- solicit or accept contributions in a manner that violates applicable law;
- offer an investment, promise financial return, or issue a token as an unregistered security;
- collect personal information without a documented lawful purpose and safeguards; or
- make or amplify an allegation as fact without sufficient evidence.

## Article VIII — Amendments and dissolution

Constitutional amendments require a 21-day comment period and a two-thirds vote. On dissolution, public repository materials remain available under their stated licenses, except content that must be removed for privacy, legal, or security reasons.
