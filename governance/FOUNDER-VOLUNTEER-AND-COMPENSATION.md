# Founder volunteer service and future compensation policy

> **Founding-stage policy model.** This is a public governance template, not legal, tax, employment, or accounting advice. It must be adopted and adapted by the organization’s authorized governing body before it is relied on.

## 1. Current volunteer service

Until a governing body has approved compensation in writing and the organization has available unrestricted operating funds, founders, organizers, and contributors serve voluntarily. Volunteer service creates no promise of salary, equity, token allocation, revenue share, deferred compensation, or retroactive payment.

This project does not represent that donations, grants, memberships, or other funds will be used to compensate a founder unless that use has been independently approved and publicly disclosed as appropriate.

## 2. Expense boundaries

Contributors should keep personal and organizational funds separate. Reimbursement is limited to reasonable, documented, mission-related expenses that an authorized person approves under a written expense policy. A founder may not approve their own reimbursement.

## 3. Preconditions for any future compensation

Before a founder, officer, director, trustee, or related person receives compensation, the organization should have:

1. an authorized, conflict-free governing body or committee;
2. a written conflict-of-interest policy;
3. a written role description, scope, and expected time commitment;
4. a budget showing that unrestricted operating funds can support the expense;
5. comparable compensation data appropriate to the role, geography, size, and financial condition of the organization; and
6. a written decision made before compensation begins, with the conflicted person fully recused.

## 4. Independent approval and documentation

The person seeking compensation must disclose the interest, leave the decision process, and not vote or approve their own pay, benefits, reimbursement, or employment terms. The independent decision-makers must record the date, participants, comparability information reviewed, compensation approved, and the rationale in contemporaneous minutes or a decision record.

An outside compensation consultant, accountant, or attorney may inform the analysis, but does not replace a decision by the entity’s authorized, conflict-free governing body.

## 5. Form and review of compensation

Any approved compensation should be prospective, fixed or objectively structured, mission-appropriate, and paid through normal payroll and tax reporting where required. It must not be calculated as a percentage of donations, fundraising proceeds, token proceeds, or grants. The governing body should review it at least annually and whenever the role or financial position materially changes.

## 6. Public accountability

The organization should disclose material compensation arrangements and related conflicts in the form and detail required by applicable law and consistent with individual privacy and safety. Financial reports should clearly distinguish program spending, administration, fundraising, contractor expenses, reimbursements, and compensation.

## 7. No ownership or private-benefit substitute

Service to this mission does not create ownership in a charitable or public-benefit entity. This policy does not authorize an ownership token, profit share, dividend, investment return, or a fundraising instrument that promises financial upside to contributors or insiders.
