# Public Funding & Smart Contracts

4PILLARS may seek public support for research, education, open-source tools, community engagement, and pilot evaluation. Money should strengthen the public record—not buy control over policy.

## Funding principles

- Support is a donation, grant, sponsorship, or service payment only when its legal terms clearly say so.
- No contribution buys a vote, preferred policy outcome, equity, token, revenue share, or investment return.
- Material funders and conflicts should be disclosed under a published policy.
- Regulated activity requires a separate, counsel-reviewed entity and process.

## Where smart contracts can help

Smart contracts are useful only when they create a concrete public benefit that ordinary tools cannot deliver as well. Potential uses include:

1. publishing time-stamped hashes of adopted policy versions and decision records;
2. multi-signature release controls for a clearly defined project budget; and
3. publicly auditable milestone attestations, with off-chain evidence linked from the repository.

## What will not be done by default

- No governance token.
- No public token sale, investment contract, yield promise, or revenue-sharing instrument.
- No contract that holds personal data, votes without identity safeguards, or automatically distributes money without legal and security review.
- No deployment before independent security review, tested recovery controls, jurisdiction-specific counsel, and a member decision record.
