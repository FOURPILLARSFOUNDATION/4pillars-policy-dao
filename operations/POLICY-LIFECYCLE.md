# Policy lifecycle

## 1. Scope the problem

Write a one-page brief identifying the jurisdiction, target population, present rule or condition, problem evidence, and the decision the policy would change.

## 2. Build an evidence dossier

Document sources, data quality, counterarguments, uncertainty, and stakeholder perspectives. A claim without a source is an assumption, not evidence.

## 3. Draft the intervention

Specify authority, definitions, duties, funding, implementation timeline, enforcement, reporting, sunset/review, and remedies. Use plain language alongside legislative language.

## 4. Run four mandatory reviews

| Review | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Legal and rights | Is it authorized? Does it protect due process, speech, privacy, equality, and property rights? |
| Fiscal and operational | Who administers it, what does it cost, and what could fail? |
| Equity and affected people | Who benefits, who pays, and who has a meaningful voice? |
| Integrity and data | Are conflicts disclosed, data minimized, and evidence traceable? |

## 5. Open comment and revise

Provide the full versioned draft, a plain-language summary, and a method to submit accessible comments. Publish a response matrix that explains material changes or why a recommendation was not adopted.

## 6. Decide and release

Use the Constitution’s decision process. Release an adopted model with an implementation checklist, source list, known limitations, and review date.

## 7. Measure, learn, and amend

Publish outcome metrics and unintended effects. A policy that cannot be evaluated has not completed its implementation cycle.
