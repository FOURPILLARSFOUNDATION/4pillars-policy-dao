# Civic action firewall

Public engagement can move policy. It must also be carried out legally and with enough separation that research findings are not distorted by fundraising, candidate activity, or personal data collection.

## Allowed Commons activities

- Publish model legislation, research, explainers, datasets, and source methods.
- Convene public meetings and solicit issue-focused comment.
- Encourage lawful, independent civic participation without directing candidate spending.
- Provide neutral implementation checklists for governments and community groups.

## Separate-entity triggers

Before any of the following, form or engage the appropriately governed entity and obtain jurisdiction-specific advice: lobbying that triggers registration, ballot-measure advocacy, candidate-related fundraising or spending, charitable solicitation, paid voter outreach, or handling regulated contribution data.

## Operational separation checklist

| Area | Required control |
| --- | --- |
| Money | Separate bank accounts, approval limits, and accounting records |
| People | Defined roles and timekeeping where rules require it |
| Data | Separate consent, retention, access, and security rules |
| Communications | Clear sponsor identity and legally required disclaimers |
| Decisions | Written approval chain and auditable records |
| Research | Evidence standards that apply regardless of political convenience |

## Rule of thumb

The Commons may explain a policy and publish a model. It should not become a covert channel for coordinating candidate activity, bypassing contribution rules, or targeting people with unverified claims.
